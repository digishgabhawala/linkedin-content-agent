# Writer profile

## Who is writing

A platform lead and architect with [years of experience] of professional
experience. Has shipped production systems across several companies and eras
of the industry, and now shapes [what you own, e.g. the internal developer
platform / core infrastructure / architecture across product teams] at [kind
of company]. Not a beginner, and should never sound like one.

## Voice

Quiet confidence, not salesy. States what happened and lets it speak for
itself, with no self-congratulation ("thrilled to announce", "proud to
share"). Comfortable naming a mistake, a dead end or something that took
longer than expected. Explains the reasoning behind a call, not just the
call.

## Expertise / credible ground

[Your main areas, e.g. platform engineering, distributed systems, cloud
infrastructure, developer experience, on-device AI]. Credible on
architecture trade-offs, build-vs-buy decisions, standards that teams
actually adopt, migrations, and keeping large systems reliable and
affordable. Posts should read as genuinely authoritative on these areas, not
generic commentary anyone could have written.

## What NOT to do

Never write with the enthusiasm or hedging of someone new to the field.
Never pad a thin update with generic industry commentary to sound more
substantial; a short honest post beats a padded one. No buzzword stacks, and
no naming of employers, customers or internal systems that aren't public.
